# Front drill (frontal analysis trainer): design card

*A training game, not a scored study. Sibling of the pattern drill (`studies/2026-11-05-pattern-drill/`). Agreed with Jason in the thread "Build a frontal analysis trainer from OPC archives", 2026-11-06 11:33-11:53Z.*

**Question** (Jason's words): "another good training tool would be frontal analysis. could we use archived OPC analysis to build a database?"; "we need the full unified surface analysis at synoptic hours ... We want to focus on one storm system so we dont need a ton of time. it should be rapid fire because we dont have forever to make decisions on the desk"; "Drawing should tap points. we need several front type selections. Scoring on the distance should be generous. scoring should be stricter on front type"; "for data, we should get MSLP, 1000-850 equivilent potential temp, IR, 250 mb wind speed".

**What it is:** a web page. Each case is one storm system at one synoptic hour, maps shown, fronts hidden. A soft dashed ring (centred on the analysed low) marks the system. The page GIVES the trainee the system's analysed low(s) (red L) and its stationary fronts (Jason 2026-11-06: "place the L for me. I just want to draw fronts ... just place the stationary already", "dont worry about troughs"); the trainee taps points along its cold, warm and occluded fronts, picks the type (three buttons), then submits. The reveal overlays the Unified Surface Analysis fronts and lows and scores the drawing. Timed, about 60-90 s a case, many cases, session stats only (no accounts), same look as the pattern drill (cream, navy, oxblood).

**Truth:** the Unified Surface Analysis (OPC, WPC, NHC) as coded by WPC in the CODSUS bulletin (`ASUS02 KWBC`, 0.1 deg points, archived by IEM). Synoptic hours only (00, 06, 12, 18 UTC): the 03/09/15/21 bulletins are not the full ocean analysis. Types carried: cold, warm, occluded, stationary, trough; highs and lows with central pressure. Checked against OPC's two Atlantic charts for 2026-11-01 12Z: fronts and lows line up (`/mnt/project-files/frontal-trainer/opc-vs-coded-fronts-2026110112Z.png`). Coverage history (it changed, so check the date): before the 18Z analysis of 2026-08-12 (`frontlib.COVERAGE_FULL_FROM`) the bulletin clipped fronts at WPC's slanted map edge, easternmost points by latitude about 63W at 30N, 41W at 40N, 29W at 45N, 23W at 50N and 24W at 55-60N (`old_edge.csv`, from `domain_edge.py`: per 1-degree band the maximum over every analysis Nov 2024-Jul 2026, 5-band median, 3-band mean); from that analysis on it covers the Atlantic to ~0W (12Z that day has 38 fronts, none beyond 260E at 28-42N; 18Z has 71 reaching 357E; odd-hour bulletins can still be partial). In the Pacific the old bulletin reached only ~150W (checked over 28 synoptic analyses, 1-7 Dec 2025: ocean lows and fronts from about 25W to about 150W). The Atlantic HSF area is covered; the Pacific is only the north-east Pacific east of ~150W (WPC's map edge; after Aug 2026 the Pacific side reaches the date line, 180, at every latitude checked: easternmost-west points 180.0-184.5E in Aug-Nov 2026; points west of 152W south of 60N are a handful of front tails, and points to 180W are Alaska and the Bering Sea). The first estimate ("to ~170W") counted those Alaska points. 0.1 deg bulletins exist from ~2011, whole-degree back to 2005.

**Fronts are plain coloured lines (Jason 2026-11-06: "leave pips off and just have colored lines so we dont have to worry about the direction")**: cold blue, warm red, occluded purple, stationary alternating red / blue dashes, trough orange dashed; no triangles or semicircles anywhere, so no symbol side to get right. Scoring unchanged. Jason also asked for wider, true-shape maps; he then said "we dont want to repull everything. i can live with this.", so the 25 x 40 deg box, the equirectangular drawing and the deck are unchanged.

**Maps** (GFS analysis f000, 0.25 deg, box coarsened to 0.5 deg like the drill; imagery kept at ~0.1 deg). Jason chose MSLP, 1000-850 theta-e, IR, 250 hPa wind speed. After the expert review (below) Jason approved this set of five views (a sixth, 10 m wind, was added 2026-11-06; the 700 hPa theta-e view became 10 m convergence the same day) (12:01Z), isobars on every one:
1. MSLP every 4 hPa with 1000-500 thickness and GFS 10 m barbs (default; ASCAT only through its checkbox, below).
2. Imagery: air-mass RGB (GOES-East in the Atlantic, GOES-West in the Pacific, `rk.satellite.airmass_box(basin, ...)`) only for a case low at or south of 55N and, in the Atlantic, inside the GOES-East disk (east of ~25W is outside it; the Pacific has no such cut, pixels past a 80 deg view angle are blank); GridSat IR otherwise (the RGB goes false purple near the disk edge at high latitude); linear greyscale +25 to -65 C, no convective enhancement; within 30 min of the hour.
3. Theta-e at 850 hPa (chosen over the 1000-850 mean: the mean smooths the gradient and the 1000 hPa level carries sea-surface fluxes).
4. 10 m convergence (Jason 2026-11-06, replacing 700 hPa theta-e: "maybe we should add something other than 700 theta-e. maybe surface convergence?"): computed IN THE PAGE from the case's u10 / v10 (no deck change): spherical divergence (du/dlon + d(v cos lat)/dlat) / (a cos lat), centred differences, 3 x 3 box mean, units 1e-5 per s, positive = converging; warm colours converge, cool diverge, white under 0.5, 1-unit bands, symmetric scale +-6 (about the 98th percentile of |convergence| within 8 deg of the case lows over the 522 cases: 5.6; deck-wide 98th 4.8), MSLP contours and GFS barbs on top so a convergence line reads against the wind shift. `frontlib.convergence` is the same maths in numpy (tested against `u = -kx`) and gives the occluded-front hint (convergence on the line against the air 250 km either side). The 700 hPa fields stay in the deck's .bin (the page ignores them).
5. 3 h pressure tendency (falls ahead of the warm front, couplet at the triple point).
6. 10 m wind (Jason: "we need the model wind field too so we can see the whole field vs ascat"): GFS 10 m speed as shading in the page's wind colours (10/15/20/25/30/34/41/48/56/64/80 kt) with GFS barbs and isobars. GFS barbs are on the default view too; ASCAT never replaces them: a checkbox "ASCAT" (shown only for cases with a pass) overlays the swath shading and navy ASCAT barbs on whichever view is showing (GFS barbs are hidden inside the swath while it is on), so the model and the scatterometer can be flipped on the 10 m wind view.
250 hPa isotachs as a checkbox overlay rather than their own map.

**Drawing:** tap points along a front, choose a type from cold / warm / occluded, finish, start the next; undo and delete. No L to place and no stationary or trough button: the system's lows and stationary fronts are drawn before the trainee starts (`givenOf` in the page; troughs are not shown until the reveal and are never scored). Works on a phone.

**Scoring** (generous on position, strict on type):
- Analysed fronts are curved through the coded points (`rk.fronts.smooth`: centripetal Catmull-Rom in a local equirectangular plane, every coded point kept, a point about every 25 nm; coded points are a median 102 nm apart; Jason: "we need some curve to the fronts between points") and the trainee's fronts get the same curve (the page draws it in screen space and scores the 25 nm-sampled geographic curve; the taps are the control points, dots while drawing); same-type segments meeting within 30 nm are merged; truth is clipped to the box minus 1 deg and fronts with under 150 nm inside are not scored.
- Only the case low's SYSTEM is scored ("The system", below): other fronts in the box are shown greyed at the reveal as "other systems, not scored" and are never missed or extra. A drawn front with no match is penalised as extra only when at least half of it lies inside the ring; fronts drawn outside the ring cost nothing.
- Matching is one to one (one drawn line cannot claim the cold, warm and occluded fronts at a triple point). Distance is measured both ways over the overlapping part; coverage (share of the analysed length within 150 nm) gets a small weight of its own.
- Position: full credit within 150 nm, sliding to none at 300 nm.
- Type: worth twice position, and only scored when the front is matched (scaled by its position credit). Half credit for occluded vs cold near the triple point and for stationary vs a weak cold or warm front; none for cold vs warm.
- Missed fronts cost the most, weighted by length and nearness to the low. What is scored: the system's cold, warm and occluded fronts. Troughs and stationary fronts are never penalised, missed or extra (troughs are 44% of coded features and drawn inconsistently; stationary fronts are given).
- Low: not scored (`SCORE.LOW_W` 0; the L is given). The scoring code is kept so a later version can ask for it again. The ring is centred on the true low (`RING_OFFSET_NM` 0) and has radius `sys_r_nm`. Stationary fronts are given, so they are not scored, missed or extra either. Triple point as its own target later.
Constants live in one place in the page so they can be tuned after forecasters play it.

**Cases (second deck, Jason 2026-11-06: "even if we work with only cases after Aug 6 2026 there should be plenty to work with. we can use the full domain over the ocean").** Only analyses from `COVERAGE_FULL_FROM` (2026-08-12 18Z; cases from the next analysis so the 6 h neighbour is also full coverage) to the last synoptic time of the latest complete UTC day (2026-11-05 18Z), 00/06/12/18Z: 340 times, 337 analyses loaded. Both basins: North Atlantic 100W to 3W, North Pacific from 180 to 116W (`find_cases.BASINS`; the first deck was Atlantic only, 100W to 25W or 10W). The coded bulletin has nothing west of 180 (checked: 279,577 coded position tokens over 13 Aug-5 Nov, none west of 180W, none east of 1W), so the Pacific cases are the ones east of the date line, and a case whose system front ends within 1.5 deg of 180 (or of 1W in the Atlantic) is dropped as clipped (`frontlib.basin_edge_clipped`). A case = an analysed low 25-67N over open water (GFS land mask connected to the open ocean of its basin, no sea ice (GFS ICEC under 0.15), not Hudson or James Bay, Gulf of Mexico, Gulf of St Lawrence, Gulf of California, Puget Sound or the Strait of Georgia: `find_cases.excluded_water`), with at least one COLD, WARM or OCCLUDED front end within ~300 km (what the trainee draws; a stationary front alone no longer qualifies); at most one case per low per UTC day. Quality filters as before: the GFS MSLP minimum within 150 km and 4 hPa of the analysed low; no second analysed low within 500 km; and the 6 h type match. All passing cases are taken, no cap, no balancing. Box 25 x 40 deg centred on the low. `basin` ("natl"/"npac") is a field of each case and the page header shows it ("N Atlantic" / "N Pacific"). Ids are g0001... (the first deck's f0001... files stay on disk beside, out of the manifest; `manifest_v1.json`, `key_v1.csv` and `picked_v1.csv` keep its lists).

Counts (2316 candidates: N Atlantic 1467, N Pacific 849; 1781 with no second low within 500 km): the STRICT 6 h match (the low's non-trough type set, stationary included, is identical in the analyses 6 h before and after) leaves 210 cases (N Atlantic 131, N Pacific 79), under the ~250 asked for, so the RELAXED match is used: every cold, warm or occluded type present now was also present (on the nearest analysed low) 6 h before or 6 h after, a missing neighbour skipped: 577 cases (N Atlantic 349, N Pacific 228) after the GFS check and one per low per day. Built 577, then dropped 4 (system under 250 nm of non-trough front, or nothing the trainee draws) and 51 (a system front ends at the edge of the coded analysis): deck 522 cases, N Atlantic 330, N Pacific 192. Imagery: Atlantic air-mass 179, IR 144, none 7 (GridSat-B1 is not published for the last days); Pacific air-mass 133, IR 57, none 2. ASCAT on 156 cases (RSS posts about 2 days late, so the last days have none). Streamed: about 12.5 GB in all (GFS about 5.9 GB and GOES ABI about 6.1 GB for the 577 built cases, scaled from the 2.2 + 1.1 GB and 2.3 + 1.2 GB measured in two of the build segments, plus about 0.45 GB of MSLP and ice crops for the case finder), well under the 50 GB limit; first-deck work files and caches reused.

**Truth cut-off (Jason, 2026-11-06: "were not getting the full real fronts. why are they cut off?").** Cause: the clipped domain before 2026-08-12 18Z (above). The second deck uses only analyses from the full-coverage domain, so the old-edge check (`frontlib.edge_clipped`, used for the first deck's 3 dropped cases) no longer matters; the new edge check is the date line and 1W.


**The system** (Jason, 2026-11-06: one storm system per case; the first deck scored every non-trough front in the 25 x 40 deg box, up to 8 fronts and 8 lows from several systems). `build_deck.py` (`frontlib.mark_system`) flags each truth front `"sys": true` when it belongs to the case low's system: a non-trough front with an end within 300 km of the low, then, repeatedly, any front (a trough too) with an end within 60 nm of a point of a non-trough system front (the cold front trailing from the triple point of an occlusion, a stationary continuation); troughs are attached this way but never extend the system. `sys_r_nm` is the farthest system point from the low plus 60 nm, capped at 900. A case is dropped when its system has under 250 nm of non-trough front after clipping. Hints are written for the system's front types only. First 2026-11-06 deck: 134 of 143 picked cases kept (9 dropped); the 191-case deck keeps 131 of the 143 (9 short, 3 at the old edge) and adds 60; scored fronts per case 1: 73, 2: 32, 3: 24, 4 or more: 5; greyed non-system fronts 0-9 per case.

**Parser rules** (`rk/fronts.py`): ASUS02 only; keep the last issue for a valid time (corrections); year from the header (VALID is MMDDHH); parse tokens, not lines (pressure/position pairs wrap); stop at `$$`; drop repeated points.

**Reveal:** OPC fronts and lows over the trainee's (the system's in full colour, other systems thin and grey, "not scored"); the hint footer says analysts draw a front on the warm edge of the temperature change and at the wind shift, not in the middle of the theta-e gradient; triple point marked; one line per front type with the case's own numbers (cold: veer and theta-e drop; warm: backing and cloud shield; occluded: 700 theta-e ridge to the low; stationary: isobars along the front; trough: kink, no thermal gradient). Shapiro-Keyser cases noted as such (the coded analysis has no bent-back type; analysts draw it occluded or warm). Session stats include a front-type confusion table.

**Expert review (2026-11-06, three reviewers: surface analyst, satellite/conceptual models, verification with 24 bulletins checked):** changes above come from it. One reviewer saw fewer lows in 12Z bulletins over three days; a 28-analysis check (1-7 Dec 2025) did not repeat it (12Z 10-25 lows vs 9-33 at other hours), so no 12Z rule, but the 6 h consistency filter would catch a dropped system.

**Data:** GFS f000 records per case time (MSLP, 1000/500 hgt, T and RH at 850/700, 250 u/v, 10 m u/v) plus MSLP 3 h earlier through `rk.fetch.gfs_global`, ~12 MB per time, 577 times in the second deck, about 12.5 GB streamed with the GOES air-mass bands (first deck: a few GB), resumable per-time caches under `rk.paths.data("extra", "front-drill")`. CODSUS text: one IEM request per day, cached. Well under 50 GB.

**Reused / new:**
- Reused: `rk.fetch.gfs_global`, `rk.diag` (theta_e, smooth, coarsen), `rk.satellite`, `rk.truth.ascat`, the drill's box, quantise, satellite and export code (`build_deck.py`, `page/export_site.py`), page skin.
- New in `rk/`: `rk/fronts.py` (fetch + parse CODSUS into fronts and pressure centres, cached; `smooth` curves; with a test), so any later study (front-relative ASCAT, frontal wind maxima) can use the analysed fronts.
- New here: `find_cases.py`, `build_deck.py`, `page/`.

**Publish:** its own folder on the drill site (jkrek17/pattern-drill, `/fronts/`) once Jason has played a private preview.

**Out of scope for v1:** whole-basin analysis mode, West Pacific (the bulletin does not code it), the Mediterranean and other enclosed seas, highs, pressure calls, squall lines and dry lines, years before 2021 (no GFS analyses on AWS; ERA5 would be needed).

## Deck format (contract between `build_deck.py` and the page)

Same box as the pattern drill: 0.5 deg, 51 x 81 cells (25 x 40 deg), centred on the analysed low; one snapshot per case (the analysis hour).

`deck/manifest.json`:
```
{"version": 1, "res": 0.5, "ny": 51, "nx": 81,
 "fields": [{"key": "msl", "name": "...", "units": "hPa", "offset": 930, "scale": 0.5}, ...],
 "cases": [{"id": "g0001", "basin": "natl"|"npac", "lat0": 62.0, "lon0": 300.0,      # north-west cell centre, lon 0-360
            "low": {"lat": .., "lon": .., "hpa": ..},          # analysed low (truth)
            "lows": [{"lat":..,"lon":..,"hpa":..}, ...],        # every analysed low in the box (twin centres)
            "fronts": [{"type": "cold"|"warm"|"occluded"|"stationary"|"trough",
                        "pts": [[lat, lon], ...],
                        "sys": true|false}, ...],           # merged, curved through the coded points (rk.fronts.smooth) at ~25 nm, clipped to the box minus 1 deg; sys: belongs to the case low's system
            "sys_r_nm": 692,                                    # ring radius around the case low, nm
            "img": "am"|"ir"|null, "ascat": true|false,
            "hints": {"cold": "...", ...}}]}                    # one line per non-trough front type of the system, numbers from the case
```
Fields in order (uint8, value = offset + scale x byte, 255 missing): `msl` (930, 0.5 hPa), `thk` (470, 0.5 dam), `u10`, `v10` (-40, 0.32 m/s), `te850` (240, 0.5 K), `te700` (240, 0.5 K), `dp3` (3 h MSLP change, -12.7, 0.1 hPa), `wspd250` (0, 1 kt), `ascat_spd` (0, 1 kt, 255 unseen), `ascat_dir` (0, 2 deg), `land` (0/1).
`img` is "am" only when the case low is at or south of 55N and the box centre is inside the GOES-East disk (not east of 25W), "ir" (GridSat IR) otherwise, null when neither exists. A manifest without `sys` / `sys_r_nm` (an older deck) is read by the page as all fronts in the system and no ring; one without `basin` is read as the Atlantic. `img` "am" is GOES-East in the Atlantic and GOES-West in the Pacific (low at or south of 55N).

The Artifact preview (`page/make_single.py`) packs the deck: `encoding: "pack"`, each case has `pack` (its file `deck/pNN.json`, 25 cases, `{id: {z: base64 of the zlib-deflated .bin, img: data URI}}`, JPEGs re-compressed at quality 50), read with `DecompressionStream`. `--max-mb 15` (default; 0 = no cap, `--max-mb 55` keeps all 522) keeps a seeded random subset with the Atlantic/Pacific split when the deck is larger: the 522-case deck is 21.4 MB packed (23 files), the preview keeps 367 cases (232 N Atlantic, 135 N Pacific; 15.0 MB, 17 files).

`deck/<id>.bin` = the fields in that order, each `ny x nx`, row 0 north. `deck/<id>_am.jpg` or `_ir.jpg`: imagery at 0.1 deg over the box's cell-edge extent (250 x 400 px), as the drill. `key.csv` (not published) maps ids to valid times.

**Site export:** `page/export_site.py <site clone>` writes `<clone>/fronts/` (index.html, app.js, style.css, deck/ with manifest and packs of the whole deck; no key.csv, nothing from work/) with the site header (Training, Wind / Fronts nav); it replaces the folder on rerun and does not touch git. `tests/test_front_drill_study.py` checks no key.csv and no absolute paths. `build_deck.py --rehint` rewrites the occluded hints of the deck's manifest from the local .bin files (no downloads).
