# The desk's 30 m wind practice against ASCAT, beside Best Wind

*2026-11-16 · Atlantic and Pacific, 00/12Z cycles, +24 to +126 h (06/18Z-valid steps included) · winter 2025-26 · held-out: 5 ISO weeks, 1,317 cases (Atlantic 700, Pacific 617) · truth: RSS ASCAT real 10 m, open-water seen cells · descriptive comparison, no pick (design.md)*

## The answer
The desk's way of using Stability Height (30 m) is a **good storm-force finder and a poor size-of-area wind**. Applying it to GFS (strict mask) and to ECMWF (loose mask) and averaging the two catches 71% of ASCAT's storm-force tiles in the Atlantic and 51% in the Pacific, against 74% and 58% for Best Wind. On storm catch the two are about level: Best Wind is 4 to 7 points ahead pooled over all leads, the desk practice is 3 points ahead at +24 h in the Atlantic, and with five held-out weeks neither gap is established (Atlantic: Best Wind ahead in all five weeks, by 1.0 to 8.5 points, and a t-interval on those five reaches zero, [-7.4, +0.2]; Pacific weekly differences run from +2.5 to -33 points). The practice overdraws gale water: 1.64x ASCAT's gale area in the Atlantic and 2.08x in the Pacific, where Best Wind draws 1.05x and 1.13x. It runs 1.4 kt (Atlantic) and 1.6 kt (Pacific) worse on the cells that matter, those where ASCAT or any grid shows 30 kt or more.

Why: the lift goes where the mask says unstable, which is most cold-sector water, and only 9% (Atlantic) and 4% (Pacific) of the lifted water ASCAT saw was gale force. Where the base wind was under 34 kt, the lifted wind runs 1.8 kt (Atlantic) and 2.3 kt (Pacific) high. Averaging the two models' lifts softens this against applying the tool to the blend (6 Nov form: gale area 1.90x / 2.54x, error 6.2 / 6.3 kt), because the plain GFS/ECMWF mean it starts from is weaker than the blend (gale area 0.75x against 0.93x); the two models' unstable masks agree on 94-96% of cells, so the masks are not the reason. The weaker base also halves the storm lift. Storm-force area comes out about right (1.01x Atlantic, 0.84x Pacific) but hurricane-force is under-drawn (0.55x / 0.24x; 33% and 20% of HF tiles caught, against 61% and 40% for Best Wind).

The 30 m step does not place the cores better. With the area forced equal to ASCAT's, storm-force cell catch is 49% for GFS and 49% for 30 m GFS in the Atlantic (ECMWF 53%, 30 m ECMWF 53%): it raises the level, not the position. Make_BestWind (GFE) sits with Best Wind (75% / 63% storm tiles at 0.95x / 1.07x gale area). The Pacific has 180 storm tiles in the five held-out weeks and only 15 at HF, so its tile numbers are thin; the +24 h and +48 h Pacific storm-tile comparisons rest on 15-16 tiles.

## What it does not show
- **How the desk actually weights the models.** Desk practice is the plain mean of the GFS and ECMWF stability winds; the forecaster's own blend percentages, and the other models in the tool's list (CMC, UKMET, NAM, ...), were not scored. Applied to GFS alone the lift is the strongest (above); the averaged result is sensitive to the weights.
- **Approximations in the tool.** GFS surface pressure is MSLP (the tool reads `p:SFC`); ECMWF uses MSLP (the tool reads `pmsl:SFC`); the mask is grown 2 cells and tapered over 3 cells (the tool's taper is 4 cells wide). The strict and loose mask forms and the x1.179 factor are as the 6 Nov score. Only "Stability Height" at 30 m was scored, not Boosted, "Entire" mode in practice (a 6 Nov-form row is in `summary.csv`), or the .995 sigma options.
- **A few kt of the error is the referee.** ASCAT reads low in the strongest cores, so part of every "high" number here, Best Wind's included, is ASCAT.
- **The comparison is not even-handed.** Best Wind's ramps and gale floor were tuned on this winter's ASCAT; the desk tool was never tuned. The held-out weeks are also not clean for Best Wind: the 80% gale floor was adopted on its held-out gale area (the same 1.05 / 1.13 reported here), and the +6 then +4 ramp was chosen after a held-out HF overshoot had been seen. Read Best Wind's area numbers as fitted, not as a blind test.
- **Speeds were averaged, not vectors.** The tool blends U and V; this study averaged the two models' speeds, which reads a little high where their directions differ. The size could not be measured (no components stored).
- Five held-out ISO weeks of one winter. The intervals resample those weeks, so they are tighter than another winter would allow. Pacific storm tiles (180) and HF tiles (15) are few; its tile intervals are wide.
- Cases: 1,330 held-out cases have page inputs (the 70 without are the +24 to +120 h steps of 14 Pacific cycles on 5-11 Jan 2026, so that Pacific held-out week has 70 cases instead of 140); 13 more Pacific cases lack lagged runs for the GFE blend. All summaries are on the 1,317 where every product was built.

## How it was done
- Truth: `rk.truth.ascat_target` (intercalibrated, rain dropped, +-90 min), open water, ASCAT-seen cells. One decision-cell set per case (ASCAT or any of the 15 grids >= 30 kt), the same for every product.
- Products: GFS and ECMWF 10 m from the store; Stability Height as `strong-core-lift/score.py desk_stability_wind` (theta925 <= theta_sfc, GFS strict with 2 m temperature, ECMWF loose with skin temperature, x(30/10)^0.15, 2-cell rim, taper); Best Wind `score_cells.best` plus the 80% gale floor the page now uses (the 60% floor of the 7 Nov study is in `summary.csv` as `best60`); Make_BestWind `gfe_score.products`. Counts: `fit_score.Tiles` (the tile counting the 6 and 7 Nov numbers use; `rk.score.tile_peaks` was not used, it scores peak errors, not catch), equal-area catch `summarize.eq_area`, bands `rk.score.band_error`, intervals `rk.compare.paired_counts` (whole ISO weeks, 2,000 draws).
- Continuity (`continuity.csv`, `check_6nov.csv`). 6 Nov tool-on-blend, strict mask: Pacific on the 6 Nov case set (687 of its 700 cases: 13 lack lagged runs) 59.9% storm tiles and gale area 2.52 against 60.1% and 2.53 published, storm area 1.3097 identical. Atlantic 76.5% and 1.90 against 75.2% and 1.88; the store now holds the low-level fields for 700 Atlantic cases where the 6 Nov run had 626, so the case sets differ and no closer match is expected. On this study's 617 Pacific common cases the same product reads 58.3% and 2.54. The 7 Nov Best Wind (60% floor) matches its committed held-out table: storm tiles 74.5% / 57.8%, gale area 1.271 / 1.383. Best Wind with the 80% floor gives gale area 1.05 Atlantic (1.13 Pacific). The storm and HF areas 0.94 and 0.87 quoted for Best Wind in the 7 Nov write-up are whole-winter numbers; on the held-out weeks they are 1.05 and 1.35.
- Build: `build_cases.py` runs the two earlier case builders in one pass over the store's cycle zips (about 10 GB downloaded from the data releases, no NOAA/ECMWF forecast pull, 15 minutes). One change to how the truth layer runs: its attempt to read an ECMWF f000 analysis at 06/18Z valid times (no such analysis exists; the read returns None after about 37 s of retries) is answered None at once, so the water mask falls back to the GFS land/ice mask exactly as it does after the retries. The GFS mask for those valid times was read from NOAA (two small messages per time).
- Code: `studies/2026-11-16-desk-30m-vs-best-wind/` (`build_cases.py`, `score.py`, `summarize.py`, `check_6nov.py`); per-case rows `RK_DATA/extra/desk-30m-vs-best-wind/case_rows.csv.gz` (all products, all counts) and `case_picks.csv.gz` here (gale area ratio and storm-tile catch per product per case, for picking worked cases).
- Checked by: an independent agent (16 Nov) recomputed every number in the table, the lead rows and the paired differences from `case_rows.csv.gz` with its own script, without reading this write-up: all matched (areas to 0.01, tiles to the count, error to 0.01 kt). It also rebuilt the desk-practice wind from the cached grids with its own mask code on 114 cases; 113 matched exactly. Its read of the draft changed four sentences (why averaging softens the lift, which Pacific cases are missing, the storm-tile count, and the strength of the storm-catch claim) and added the two caveats on tuning and speed averaging. Its sensitivity checks: the tool's 4-cell taper lowers desk gale area by about 0.04; a 2 hPa surface pressure shift on GFS moves it about 0.03.
